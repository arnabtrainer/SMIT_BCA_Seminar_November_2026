# 🧠 NotebookLM Demonstration Prompts
## AI Can Help Me Learn — Stay Smart in the Digital World

This prompt library uses a single notebook with complementary sources: the Fundamentals PDF, fictional Cases DOCX, Awareness PPTX, Quick Reference TXT, CISA YouTube video, and FTC article.

**Usage:** Select the relevant imported source(s), paste a prompt, read the result, and check any citations or original materials. Studio outputs such as audio, video, slides, quizzes and mind maps may be created using their **Studio controls** rather than only a chat prompt. Names of options may vary by interface and account.

## 📄 01 — Source summary

> Summarise the main learning points across the six sources under the headings **Recognise, Protect, Verify, Respond**. Give six concise takeaways, identify which source supports each, and separate hypothetical case examples from real public guidance.

## 💬 02 — Source-grounded Q&A

> According to the *Digital Safety Fundamentals* PDF and the FTC webpage, what should someone do after receiving an unexpected account-verification link? Give a step-by-step explanation, cite the relevant passages, and say clearly if the sources leave any uncertainty.

**Verification check:** Open a citation and confirm that the wording and context actually support the claim.

## 🧠 03 — Mind map

**Studio:** Generate a Mind Map after selecting all six sources. Proposed focus: **Digital Safety → Threats → Protective Habits → Verification → Response → Digital Trust**. Open several nodes and compare them with the PDF and DOCX cases.

## 📝 04 — Quiz

**Studio:** Generate a beginner-friendly quiz covering message verification, passwords/MFA, document permissions, and AI-generated impersonation. Try different difficulty settings if available and discuss at least one answer explanation.

## 🗂️ 05 — Flashcards

**Studio:** Generate a flashcard deck using definitions from the TXT glossary and examples from the DOCX. Prioritise phishing, smishing, MFA, verification, data minimisation, passkey, voice cloning and least-privilege sharing.

## ✅ 06 — MCQ question bank (custom chat output)

> Create a **12-question multiple-choice question bank** based only on my selected sources. Mix definitions and practical scenarios. For every question provide A–D options, exactly one best answer, a 1–2 sentence explanation, and a source citation. Avoid repeating the same concept. Mark fictional scenarios clearly. At the end provide a compact answer key. Do not invent facts missing from the sources.

## 📖 07 — Glossary (custom chat output)

> Build a beginner-friendly alphabetical glossary of **20 digital-safety terms** grounded in the uploaded sources. Give a plain-English definition, a 5–10 word example or usage, and an appropriate citation. Highlight terms that come only from the TXT source. If a term is absent from the sources, do not invent a definition and pretend it was sourced.

## 🎧 08 — Audio Overview

**Studio:** Choose **Audio Overview**. Suggested focus: *Two presenters explain how to spot an online scam, why MFA matters, and what the fictional parcel case teaches*. Choose an appropriate language and length. Generate and check audio in advance; play a short saved excerpt during a seminar.

## 🎬 09 — Video Overview

**Studio:** Choose **Video Overview**. Suggested focus: *Pause → Verify → Decide: four common situations and safe responses*. Review any customisation settings for audience and visual style. Pre-generate and check the result; a Video Overview explains your sources and is **not** the same as the independent creative-film generation in Module 3.

## 🎨 10 — Infographic

**Studio:** Choose **Infographic**. Proposed output: **Five Digital Safety Habits** — recognise phishing, unique passwords, MFA, updates, and independent verification. Prefer a clean, high-contrast design. Check every label and claim against the input.

## 📊 11 — Slide deck

**Studio:** Choose **Slide Deck**. Focus: *Digital Safety at Home, in Study and at Work*. Ask for a concise, accessible presentation of the four main practices, the fictional parcel and voice scenarios, and independent verification. Check that it does not imply the fictional cases are real incidents.

## 📑 12 — Report / briefing

**Studio:** Choose **Report**, selecting an appropriate report or briefing format. Proposed topic: *Digital Safety Essentials: Risks, Practical Safeguards, and Lessons from Six Fictional Scenarios*. Ask for a short evidence-based report with an executive summary, findings, source references, uncertainties and actionable recommendations.

## 🔍 Cross-source questions that reveal NotebookLM's value

1. What does the CISA video emphasise that is **not** prominent in the case-study DOCX?
2. Compare the general recommendations in the PDF with the FTC's phishing guidance. Where do they agree?
3. Use the TXT glossary to explain the terms in Case C05. What does the material say about familiar-sounding voices?
4. Identify the source that gives the specific fictional parcel-message details. Do **not** attribute invented case details to FTC or CISA.
5. List any claims made in the awareness PPTX that cannot be supported by the other sources. If none are demonstrable, say so.

## 🛡️ Source-grounding standard

- Choose the specific sources to use; references change when selection changes.
- Open and inspect citations; a citation does not guarantee correct interpretation.
- Watch for differences between **original fiction**, **educational summaries**, and **independent public guidance**.
- Avoid invented URLs, fabricated reports, or unsupported statistics.
- Do not place private, identifiable or restricted data in demonstration sources.

The Studio features available may vary by region, plan and product updates. If a specific Studio action is unavailable, show a verified prepared output without claiming it was generated live.
