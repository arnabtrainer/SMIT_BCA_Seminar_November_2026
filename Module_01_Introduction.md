# Module 1 — Discover the Power of AI

**Seminar:** AI with Applications — Learn, Create & Build Your Future  
**Audience:** Prospective BCA applicants, newly admitted first-year students, and some senior BCA/AI-ML students  
**Target time:** 15 minutes  
**Priority:** Essential

## Purpose
Make AI understandable and exciting without assuming any coding, mathematics, or prior AI knowledge. Prepare students to experience three practical applications of AI.

## Learning outcomes
- Recognise everyday examples of AI and explain AI in ordinary language.
- Distinguish, at a very high level, traditional software from AI tools that learn patterns or generate content.
- Understand that prompts improve when they include context and an intended outcome.
- Recognise that AI is useful but can be wrong and needs human judgment.

## Proposed flow (15 minutes)
1. **0–3 min — Hook:** Ask: “Who has used AI this week—and for what?” Take two or three replies.
2. **3–7 min — AI around us:** Search, phone cameras, maps, recommendations, voice assistance, and studying. Explain with familiar student-life examples.
3. **7–10 min — What AI can do:** Learn from information, create content, help make plans, and assist in building tools. Avoid model architectures and technical jargon.
4. **10–13 min — Better prompting:** Contrast a vague request (“Teach me computers”) with a contextual request (“Explain what a computer network is to a new BCA student using a campus example”).
5. **13–15 min — Roadmap:** Introduce **Learn → Create → Build**, and explain the three upcoming projects.

## Participation
One show-of-hands poll and one audience-submitted example prompt.

## Preparation
- One welcome slide, one “AI in everyday life” slide, and one “Learn / Create / Build” roadmap slide.
- A tested prompt ready to demonstrate if audience participation is limited.

## Shortening / extension
- **Short version (10 min):** Remove the prompt comparison live run; explain it verbally.
- **Extended version (20 min):** Add a student-generated prompt and quick response comparison.

## Trainer reminder
Treat AI as a helpful assistant, not a magical source of truth. Do not teach abstract algorithms here. Aim to build curiosity rather than test existing knowledge.
