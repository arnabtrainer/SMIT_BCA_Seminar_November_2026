STAY SMART IN THE DIGITAL WORLD
Digital Safety Quick Reference and Glossary
====================================================

PURPOSE
This plain-text reference helps you recall important terms and actions.
It complements the Fundamentals PDF, fictional Casebook DOCX, awareness
slides, and current external guidance. It is not a substitute for checking
an organisation's policy or an official source.

PART B — GLOSSARY (definitions simplified for general audiences)

Authentication: Checking that a person or system is who they claim to be.
Authorisation: Deciding what an authenticated account may access or do.
Backup: A separate copy of data kept for recovery after loss or damage.
Data minimisation: Collecting or sharing only information necessary for a task.
Deepfake: Synthetic or altered media that realistically depicts someone saying or doing something.
Domain: The identifying name in a web address, such as example.org.
Encryption: Transforming information so authorised parties can read it with the proper key.
Impersonation: Pretending to be another person or organisation.
Malware: Software intended to cause harm, gain unauthorised access, or disrupt systems.
MFA: Sign-in using multiple distinct factors, adding protection beyond a password.
Passkey: A sign-in credential using cryptographic methods, often unlocked on a device.
Password manager: Software designed to generate, save, and retrieve account credentials.
Permission: An access right granted to a user, application, or link.
Phishing: Deceptive messages designed to obtain information or induce risky action.
Privacy: Appropriate control over collection, use, and sharing of personal information.
QR code: A machine-readable image encoding data such as a web address.
Recovery code: A security-sensitive code used to regain access to an account.
Security update: A software change intended to fix weaknesses or defects.
Smishing: Phishing delivered through SMS or similar text messages.
Social engineering: Manipulating people into disclosing information or taking unsafe actions.
Source citation: A reference to the document or passage supporting a claim.
Verification: Independently checking whether a claim or identity is authentic.
Voice cloning: Technology that can synthesize speech resembling a particular voice.

PART C — SAFE / UNSAFE DECISION PAIRS

Unsafe: Reply to an unexpected message with a login code.
Safer: Open the trusted app yourself and check account activity.

Unsafe: Reuse the same password across several services.
Safer: Use distinct credentials, supported by a password manager.

Unsafe: Forward confidential notes to a public link-sharing location.
Safer: Use least-privilege sharing with approved collaborators.

Unsafe: Trust a voice message merely because it sounds familiar.
Safer: Check through a separate, previously known contact method.

Unsafe: Treat any AI-generated answer as unquestionable.
Safer: Follow citations back to original sources and inspect their context.
